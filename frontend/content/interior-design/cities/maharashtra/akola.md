## Akola's homes and households

Akola Municipal Corporation had 425,817 residents in the 2011 Census{{f:akola-census-pop}}, in 85,027 households{{f:akola-hh-total}}. Living rooms, bedrooms, dining, drawing and study rooms count as dwelling rooms; kitchens, bathrooms, store rooms and verandahs do not{{f:census-room-definition}}.

| Dwelling rooms per household | Share of Akola households |
|---|---|
| One room, or no exclusive room{{f:akola-hh-small}} | 40.3% |
| Two rooms{{f:akola-hh-two}} | 28.2% |
| Three or more rooms{{f:akola-hh-large}} | 31.5% |

A little over two in five households lived in one room or had none of their own{{f:akola-hh-small}}, and just under a third had three or more{{f:akola-hh-large}}. Ownership was high, at 72.3%{{f:akola-hh-owned}}, with 24.0% renting{{f:akola-hh-rented}}.

So many Akola projects are owners fitting out a compact home they intend to keep. The priorities follow from that: storage that lasts, furniture that does more than one job, and finishes that cope with long, very dry summers.

## Extreme dryness and summer heat

The India Meteorological Department's 1991–2020 normals for Akola's observatory show 783.4 mm of rain a year over about 45 rainy days{{f:akola-imd-rain}}.

| Season | What the IMD data shows | What it means for interiors |
|---|---|---|
| Hot season{{f:akola-imd-heat,akola-imd-humidity}} | May averages a daily high of 42.4 °C; April evenings average 19% humidity | Solid wood splits, veneers lift, fabrics bleach, dust everywhere |
| Monsoon{{f:akola-imd-monsoon,akola-imd-humidity}} | 681.3 mm from June to September, about 87% of the year's rain; July is the wettest month at 215.0 mm; morning humidity of 80% in July and 83% in August | Boards near water swell; cupboards need airflow |
| Winter{{f:akola-imd-winter}} | December nights average 13.5 °C | Dry, settled weather for painting and polishing |

Air that dry pulls moisture out of anything made of wood{{f:akola-imd-humidity}}. A few months later the monsoon puts it back{{f:akola-imd-monsoon}}. That repeated shrinking and swelling is the main reason cheap furniture fails early here.

**Let boards settle before cutting.** Ask the workshop to store plywood on site for a few days, especially between March and May, so it adjusts to local air before it is cut.

**Avoid large solid-wood panels.** Use solid wood for small trims or legs if you like the look, but build doors and large panels from engineered board.

**Shade west and south windows.** Reflective-backed blinds or thermal-lined curtains reduce the afternoon heat that builds up in May.

## Cooling and storage in compact rooms

In a one- or two-room home, every summer fix has to share space with storage.

**Plan AC and fan positions first.** Fix where the indoor unit, outdoor unit and ceiling fan go before designing wardrobes or a false ceiling, so piping and drainage run in concealed channels.

**Keep the floor clear.** A wall-mounted desk with floating shelves gives a child a study corner without a bulky table.

{{img:desk}}

**Build storage to the ceiling.** Full-height wardrobes with a loft section put suitcases and winter blankets out of the way and leave the room feeling larger.

**Close it up.** Shutters and glass-fronted units instead of open shelves cut down on daily dusting through the dry months.

**Light colours on walls and ceilings.** They reflect heat and make a small room look bigger.

**Use the bed for storage.** A hydraulic storage bed holds bedding and out-of-season clothes in space the room already gives up.

## Materials that handle dry heat

In Akola the main test for any material is how it behaves when the air is extremely dry for weeks at a time, and then turns humid when the rains arrive. Choose for that cycle first.

- **Engineered boards with sealed edges.** Edge banding on all sides, backs included, so panels lose and gain moisture evenly.
- **Laminate or acrylic shutters.** They resist heat and fading better than veneer and wipe clean of dust.
- **Moisture-resistant plywood under sinks and in bathrooms.** A boiling-water-resistant or boiling-water-proof grade for the few areas that stay wet.
- **Tile or stone floors.** They stay cooler underfoot than wood in May.
- **Fade-resistant fabrics.** Use sheers or blinds in front of upholstery and curtains.
- **Roof insulation on top floors.** A reflective coating or insulation above the ceiling makes top-floor rooms far more comfortable in summer.

## Budgets and quotes in Akola

Akola district had a per capita income of ₹1,97,334 in 2023-24, 17th of the 34 district figures in Maharashtra's Economic Survey{{f:akola-district-income}}. On a tighter budget, careful comparison of quotes matters more than ever.

**Spend where you feel it daily.** Storage, a working kitchen and good shading come before decorative panelling.

**Compare quotes on identical drawings.** Every line should give the unit size, board grade and brand, shutter finish, hardware brand and countertop.

**Price services separately.** AC wiring, roof insulation, painting and electrical changes should each have their own line.

**Hold back part of the payment.** Link instalments to drawings, delivery, installation and the closed snag list.

**Ask what the warranty covers**, in writing, and whether it includes cracking, warping or lifting edges.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my furniture crack in Akola's summer?

The air is extremely dry. April evenings average 19% humidity at the city's IMD observatory{{f:akola-imd-humidity}}, so wood loses moisture and shrinks. Engineered boards with sealed edges are much steadier.

### How can I keep a small home cooler in May?

Shade west and south windows, choose light wall colours and tile floors, and insulate the roof if you live on the top floor. May averages a daily high of 42.4 °C{{f:akola-imd-heat}}.

### Where should I start in a one-room home?

With storage. In 2011, 40.3% of Akola households had one dwelling room or none of their own{{f:akola-hh-small}}, so a storage bed, full-height wardrobes and wall-mounted shelves usually come first.

### Which months suit painting and polishing?

The cooler winter months. December nights average 13.5 °C{{f:akola-imd-winter}}, and site finishes cure evenly in that weather.

### Is solid wood ever a good choice here?

For small parts such as trims, legs or handles, yes. For doors and large panels, engineered board with sealed edges holds up better to the dry heat.
